1 · NPC foresight around rock

What was wrong

A fighter only noticed a wall when its straight braking ray reached it: about 1.5 s of warning. Turning a combat-speed hull away needs about 2.5 s in this flight model, so it braked to a standstill, turned, accelerated toward its target and met the next rock.

What it does now

  • Looks as far ahead as its hull needs to turn away (turn rate and slip of that hull).
  • Predicts the curved path it can really fly from its momentum and prefers exits it can reach at speed.
  • Checks its live attack line 10× a second and re-routes as soon as that line meets rock, instead of pulsing the brakes against it.
  • Braking itself stays on the cautious straight momentum ray (predicted-path braking caused wall contacts).

2 · Two ways to turn back

The flip-and-burn stays exactly as it was: the nose swings round while the ship coasts, then the burner kills the old momentum, passing through a near stop. New beside it, the afterburner Immelmann: a wide half loop sized to the hull (thrust only acts along the nose, so a speed-keeping loop needs a radius near v²/a), keeping most of its speed and rolling out on the firing line. Aces with mid-range guns alternate the two; magrail snipers and scatter/ion brawlers keep the flip, which suits their range.

Each manoeuvre's own steering law flown through the real flight integrator (Mercury hull, target 180 m behind). Side view; trails coloured by speed as above.

3 · Hits and explosions

Hits by weapon and surface

  • Pulse / beam: molten sparks spray off the struck plate (mirrored about the surface) and a cooling glow stays on the hull for a second.
  • Shield: a cool splash, sparks skittering across the shell; no scorch.
  • Magrail: spall punched out of the far side along the shot, back-spray, shock ring.
  • Ion: electric arcs crawling over the plate, re-striking.
  • Plasma mortar / missile: big bloom, slow ember fountain, broad ring.
  • PDC / scatter: small pops.

Explosions in four kinds

  • Fireball: flash, rolling fire, smoke.
  • Chain reaction: secondaries cook off across the hull (more on capital ships).
  • Core breach: blue-white flash, wide shock ring in a tilted plane.
  • Break-up: blasts at both ends of the hull.

All add hot spark streaks and burning debris trailing fire, and keep the dying ship's momentum. Sparks are now motion streaks cooling from white-hot, not square dots. Every burst is one GPU draw, no dynamic lights. Distant bolts keep their screen size and distant muzzle flashes grow, so incoming fire reads at range.
