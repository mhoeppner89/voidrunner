# Arena Run progression

The 25-level run uses at most three authored groups per level. Encounters build through pilot skill, equipment, escort composition and capital-ship objectives.

## Encounter rules

1. **Groups, not one wave.** A level is a list of groups.
   - **When the next group arrives:** once the groups already in play are down to **one survivor**, or after **60 s** (45 s in Act I), whichever comes first. An authored timer or objective event can also trigger the group. The first eligible trigger wins, with at least **15 s between group arrivals**.
   - **Warning:** about 5 s before a group arrives, the existing reinforcement marker shows its entry and a two-second navy and amber card appears on the own-ship monitor. The warning is logged, BREAK takes priority, and pilots can continue speaking. The group uses the marked entry even if the pilot approaches it. An obstruction shifts the arrival to the nearest clear spot.
   - **Where it enters:** groups use the stage's reinforcement entries.
2. **What is in the air matters more than the total.** Later levels add better pilots, heavier ships and stronger escorts.
   - At most **three hostile fighters** at a time until Act V. At most four in Act V, the frigate and launched fighters excluded.
   - In Act V, at most two aces at a time.
   - A group whose arrival would break these limits waits until it fits, even past its timer.
3. **The curve.**
   - Difficulty rises within each act. The first level after a decision stage (new hull or wingman) is a breather.
   - Each act's last level is its peak, and the final level is the hardest of the run.
   - The endurance panel measures median combat time over the three seeds below.

     | Act | Previous build | Three-wave target |
     |---|---|---|
     | I | 0:45–1:30 | 0:45–1:15 |
     | II | 1:00–1:45 | 1:00–1:30 |
     | III | 1:15–2:00 | 1:15–1:45 |
     | IV | 1:30–2:15 | 1:30–2:00 |
     | V, levels 21–24 | 1:45–2:30 | 1:30–2:15 |
     | Level 25 | 3:00–4:30 | 3:00–4:30 |

   - First contact uses two groups. Every level has at most three groups in total, including the two follow-up escort screens at the final frigate. Red Njord's Balor reinforcements travel in its authored groups. Red Brigid and Skidbladnir launch one fighter per intact hangar and replace losses after 30 seconds.
4. **Objectives** appear on levels 4 (Red Njord Barrage, since 0.8.2pw), 5, 8, 10, 13, 15 and 25. When an objective is decided, the remaining hostiles break off and jump out within 10 s. They never surrender; that rule holds in Arena.
   - **Failing an objective does not end the run.** The level still counts as cleared, but its reward pick is skipped. Losing the pilot still ends the run, as today.
   - **Objective status on screen:** the objective banner, status strip, ticker and comms log show at the start, at 60/30/10 s left where a timer applies, and with the result. The objective target can be selected as usual.
5. **Big ships** use the corvette and frigate rules already in the game: subsystem damage, generator and engine subtargets, markers.
   - Level 9 uses the Brigid tender and its two physical repair drones. Level 10 uses Red Brigid, whose four Balors fly out of individually targetable hangars. Level 15 uses Skidbladnir and its six Loki hangars. All three hulls have three engines and two shield generators. Both carrier objectives require the carrier and its deployed fighters.

The Barrage arrives alongside its second Loki. Red Brigid calls a Balor pair at 60% hull or 35 seconds. Heimdall calls its Odin and Tyr at 65% hull or 40 seconds. The Act V picket opens with four fighters. Its 30-second relief group can arrive while two of those fighters are still alive.

Later guard flights carry shield boosters or armour and a full bank of guns. They engage a live wingman on arrival, leaving the strike leader to press the player. Bellona batteries carry mortars and some mount a lead gauss cannon. The 25th level retains its original frigate and two escort screens.

## Measuring

Use the same three seeds (175, 718, 941), pilot hull, pulse fit and scheduled wingmen on both builds. Each sample starts in a freshly seeded session with its original terrain geometry. Run the engage probe once with endurance replenishment and once with ordinary health. Record combat duration, incoming damage, maximum simultaneous hostiles and the objective result for every sortie. Report median duration and damage, the maximum hostile count, and the number of pilot losses.

```sh
PILOT=engage OUT=output/arena-progression/new-endurance.json node tools/playtest/probes/arena-progression.mjs
PILOT=engage LETHAL=1 OUT=output/arena-progression/new-flight.json node tools/playtest/probes/arena-progression.mjs
```

Set `VR_BASE_URL` to the checkout being measured. The survival target is at least 60 of 75 ordinary-health sorties completed with the pilot alive. A failed escort or intercept still advances the run.

| Levels | Maximum ordinary-health pilot losses on three seeds |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | Zero, except up to one each on 5, 8 and 10 |
| 11–20 | One per level |
| 21–24 | Two per level, with the average increase limited to one loss per level |
| 25 | Three, as on the previous build |

A Drover on a jump run keeps at least half cruise while either engine works. On the defend objective each engine retains at least one hull point. Hits still damage the ship and its destruction fails the escort, whoever fired.

Tune group contents against these measurements. Check that pressure builds through each act, that levels 6, 11, 16 and 21 give the pilot a breather, and that each finale deals the most damage in its act. Level 25 should deal the most damage of the run.

**Level 4 exception (user, 0.8.2qb):** the Barrage's flak deals double damage. The progression probe's pilot never reacts to BREAK, so at ordinary health it dies on level 4 on all three seeds (endurance still clears, absorbing about 630–670). A pilot who breaks on the cue takes no flak damage. The user chose to keep level 4 as a skill check, so Act I's zero-loss target does not apply to it.
